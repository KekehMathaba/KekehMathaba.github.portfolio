PROFESSIONAL DEVELOPMENT

Learning by doing.

A reflection on my development as an aspiring IT professional through ITPP5112.

What I’ve learned

During IT Professional Practice ITPP5112, I have learned that preparing for a career in IT involves more than building technical knowledge. It also means presenting my work clearly, thinking about the professional image I create online and reflecting honestly on the skills I still need to develop. Creating this portfolio has helped me connect those ideas. I have had to consider what a potential employer or lecturer should understand about my interests, current learning and future direction, while organising that information into a site that is easy to navigate.

A strength I’m building on

One of my current strengths is persistence when working through a problem. Programming practice has shown me that a first attempt may not work, and that I need to slow down, examine the steps and make changes carefully. I am learning to approach tasks in smaller parts rather than treating a challenge as one large obstacle. This persistence supports my interest in software development and gives me a useful base for continuing to learn.

A skill I want to improve

I want to become more confident and precise when explaining technical work. Clear communication matters when sharing ideas, documenting a solution or collaborating with people who may have different levels of technical knowledge. I plan to practise by writing short project summaries, keeping notes on my decisions and asking for feedback on how clearly I explain my work.

My next steps

I will keep strengthening my programming foundations through regular practice and coursework, while gradually exploring cloud services such as AWS, automation and DevOps. I also plan to use feedback to improve how I describe what I have learned and how I approach technical problems.

How this portfolio will help

This portfolio gives me one place to show progress over time. I can add new projects, describe what I learned from them and update my professional interests as they develop. It also gives me practice communicating my strengths without overstating my experience. My next step is to keep learning consistently, make project details more specific as my work grows and improve both the work itself and how I present it.
